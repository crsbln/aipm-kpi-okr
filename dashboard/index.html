Why small counts mislead MOCK

5Assumptions and validation questions

Mock values

  • All counts come from make_mock_data.py.
  • Invented settings: traffic, district shares, agent counts, and the view, click, lead, label, flag and complaint rates.
  • The size of the matching uplift is an invented setting, not a finding.
  • All thresholds are placeholders.

Missing baselines

  • K1: conversion under today's random allocation.
  • K2: renewal rate of matched comparison agents.
  • K3: relevance under today's random allocation.
  • K4, K5: current flag, review and complaint volumes.

Validation questions

  • What marks a lead as qualified, and what happens to unlabelled leads?
  • Does the relevance rule agree with human reviewers?
  • Is a split between random and matching allowed in the pilot?
  • How many leads are needed before a rule may fire?
  • Which complaint level is a red line?
  • Is the pilot scope (Berlin, rental, 8 weeks) agreed?

6Decision brief

Recommendation

Run the pilot before deciding. Agree targets and red lines before week 1. Measure baselines in weeks 1-2. Compare random with matching in weeks 3-8. Decide at the week-8 launch gate: scale, change, or stop.

Trade-offs

  • Waiting 8 weeks delays revenue, but avoids scaling an unproven product.
  • Matching may raise relevance and concentrate exposure on fewer agents.
  • Week 8 proves lead value at best. Renewal, the business proof, comes later.

Next steps

  1. Product lead confirms the pilot scope, design and KPI owners.
  2. Data analyst checks which events exist in the data.
  3. Sales and agents agree the qualified-lead signal.
  4. Replace every MOCK value with a measured one.
